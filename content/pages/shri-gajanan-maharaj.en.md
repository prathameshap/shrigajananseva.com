On 23 February 1878, in the town of Shegaon in Maharashtra, two local gentlemen came upon a young man by the roadside.

He was, in the words handed down to us, "a youth, who was almost all bare with radiant face, picking up particles from leftover food" that had been thrown out after a meal. He drank water left for cattle. To anyone watching he would have looked deranged. The two men looked again, and saw something else.

That day is observed as Pragat Din — the day Maharaj became visible to the world. He was not born in Shegaon and gave no account of where he came from. He simply appeared, fully formed, indifferent to the categories by which people are usually sorted.

## Thirty-two years

Maharaj remained in Shegaon for thirty-two years.

In that time an extraordinary number of accounts accumulated around him. He healed leprosy. He revived a dog that had died. He sat calmly in the middle of a fire and was untouched by it. He pacified a horse no one else could approach. Devotees came with every kind of difficulty — illness, debt, grief, doubt — and left steadied.

The miracles are the part of the story most easily told, and the part Maharaj himself cared least about. They were never performed for display. They happened around him the way weather happens around a mountain.

## What he taught

> No being is separated from God; God exists in all and all exist in God.

This was the centre of it. Maharaj taught that God is present in each and every person and in all things around us, and that the only honest response to this is to treat every being — without exception, without ranking — as equal and deserving of respect.

The rest followed from it:

- **He had no patience for pretense.** Outward show of piety without inward truth he simply ignored.
- **He named ego as the great obstacle.** Not desire, not ignorance — ego. The conviction of being separate and important is what stands between a person and what is already there.
- **He asked for humility, good deeds, devotion and righteousness.** Four ordinary things, available to anyone, requiring no learning and no lineage.

He gave no elaborate system. To those who asked him for a practice he gave a nam — **Gan Gan Ganat Bote** — and that was sufficient.

## Samadhi

Maharaj took Samadhi on 8 September 1910, on the day of Rishi Panchami. He chose the day himself.

Devotees mark the anniversary not as a bereavement but as a completion: a life that had been given away entirely, finishing exactly when and how it was meant to.

## Shri Gajanan Vijay Grantha

The life of Maharaj was set down by Saint Dasaganu Maharaj — a disciple of Shri Sai Baba of Shirdi, and a poet of real gift — in the **Shri Gajanan Vijay Grantha**, a devotional text of twenty-one chapters.

It follows Maharaj from his arrival in Shegaon through to his Samadhi. It is the text devotees read during parayan, and the reason the accounts above have survived in the form they have.

The Grantha is available in our library, along with the stotras and aartis recited daily at the mandir.
