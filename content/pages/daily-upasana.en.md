Upasana simply means sitting near. Everything below is a way of doing that, and none of it is compulsory.

If you are starting out, do not try to do all of it. Pick one thing — the evening nam jaap is the usual choice — and let the rest arrive in its own time.

## The order

The upasana follows the same sequence at the mandir and at home:

1. **Achaman and sankalp.** Sipping water, settling, and stating quietly what the practice is for.
2. **Bhupali.** The waking song, at first light.
3. **Shri Gajanan Stotra.** The stotra in praise of Maharaj.
4. **Shriram Vandana.** Vandana to Shri Ram.
5. **Nam jaap.** Gan Gan Ganat Bote, for as long as you wish.
6. **Aarti.** Madhyan in the middle of the day, Dhoop in the evening.
7. **Prarthana and prasad.** The closing prayer, then the offering is shared.
8. **Shejarati.** The last aarti, as Maharaj is laid to rest.

The complete text of each, in Devanagari with transliteration, is in the **Upasana Booklet** in our library.

## At home

You need almost nothing. A clean space, a picture or image of Maharaj if you have one, a lamp if you wish.

Face east if that is convenient and ignore it if it is not. Maharaj was not interested in ceremony for its own sake, and a practice you keep imperfectly is worth more than one you abandon because you could not do it properly.

If you cannot read Marathi, use the transliteration in the booklet, or simply listen to the recordings in our library and follow along. Many devotees listened for months before they joined in.

## Online, together

The Ram Raksha and Maruti Stotra recitation followed by nam jaap happens on Zoom every morning, and is the easiest possible entry point — you need a link and half an hour, and nobody will ask you to speak.

The same Zoom link is used every day. Save it once.

## Shrungar Seva

Abhishek and adornment of the murti is offered in person at the mandir, and devotees may take this seva by arrangement. Speak to a trustee on a darshan day, or email us.

## Dashakapurti Sadhana Sankalp Seva

Some devotees take a one-year sankalp — a commitment to a defined daily practice sustained for a full year.

Virtual sevas can be offered from anywhere, at any time of the day, so distance is not an obstacle. If you are considering taking the sankalp, speak to a trustee first; it is meant to be undertaken with guidance rather than alone.

## If you miss it

You have not failed at anything. Begin again tomorrow, without reproach. That returning is itself the practice.
