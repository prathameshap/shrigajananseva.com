Shri Gajanan Seva is a registered 501(c)(3) non-profit organisation in the Bay Area, California. It exists to offer seva to Shri Gajanan Maharaj of Shegaon, and to give the people in this valley who follow him somewhere to do that together.

There is no paid staff. Everything described on this website — the aartis, the Thursday Mahaprasad, the food and blanket drives, this website itself — is done by volunteers who have day jobs. Every dollar donated goes into the activity it was given for.

## What happens here

The centre sits in a quiet business park on Paragon Drive in north San Jose, which turns out to be an unexpectedly good place for it: the neighbouring offices empty out exactly when we fill up.

Thursday is the long day. The doors open before dawn at 6.45am for Bhupali Aarti, the shrungar is built through the morning, and the day runs through to Shejarati at 8.45pm with a full Mahaprasad served to everybody present at 8pm. Saturday and Sunday are quieter — darshan through the day, afternoon aarti at 12.10pm, and evening aarti at 5.45pm.

And every single day of the year, at 7pm Pacific, twenty-five minutes of Shri Ram Raksha Stotra and Maruti Stotra on Zoom. That session has not been missed. Devotees join from San Jose and Fremont, and from Pune, London and Sydney where it is the small hours.

## The teaching we try to keep

Shri Gajanan Maharaj left almost nothing in writing and asked for nothing to be built in his name. What he taught, he taught by doing.

He was first seen at Shegaon sitting beside a heap of discarded leaf-plates, eating the grains of rice left on them. Asked why, he said the grain had been grown by somebody's labour and it was not his to waste. **Do not waste food.** It is the single teaching most often repeated about him, and the reason a volunteer will quietly ask you to finish what is on your plate at Mahaprasad.

**Help each other** is the other. He gave no philosophy to accompany it. In practice, for us, it looks like a food drive in December, blankets before the cold, backpacks before the school year, and hygiene kits packed on a folding table by a dozen people who mostly do not know each other's surnames.

> Gan Gan Ganat Bote.

Six syllables, repeated. He gave no initiation, set no qualification and charged no fee. There is nothing to join and nobody to ask permission from. That is the whole practice, and it is why this organisation has members of no kind — only people who turn up.

## Community seva

Through the year the sangha runs drives that have nothing to do with the mandir and everything to do with the teaching:

- A **food drive** each December, when Santa Clara County food banks are shortest
- **Winter blankets** — new only, because a shelter cannot accept used bedding
- A **clothes drive** in spring
- **School supplies**, distributed to San Jose families before the school year starts
- **Hygiene kits** packed at the centre and handed out through shelters and street outreach

None of these are large by the standards of a professional charity. They are, however, entirely real, and they are done by the same twenty or thirty people who also cook the Thursday meal.

## How the organisation is run

Shri Gajanan Seva is a California non-profit corporation with 501(c)(3) status, governed by a volunteer board of trustees. No trustee is paid. The board approves the annual budget and the use of each restricted fund, and financial records are reviewed annually.

If you want to see how the money moves, that is published on the [transparency page](/about/transparency) — and if something you need is not there yet, write to us and we will publish it.

## Everyone is welcome

This needs saying plainly, because people assume otherwise and then do not come.

You do not need to be Hindu. You do not need to speak Marathi. You do not need to know when to stand, what to do with your hands, or what any of it means. There is no entry fee, no register, no membership and no dress code that anybody enforces. You can walk in on a Sunday afternoon, sit at the back for ten minutes, eat something, and leave without speaking to a single person.

That is a completely ordinary visit, and several people make one every week.
