Shri Gajanan Seva is a registered 501(c)(3) non-profit organisation in the Bay Area, California.

Inspired by the teachings of Shri Gajanan Maharaj, our mission is to foster a community rooted in seva — selfless service — and spiritual connection. We take principles that are centuries old and apply them to the needs of the place we actually live in: a valley of enormous wealth that still has neighbours sleeping outdoors in December.

## What seva means here

Seva is not a fundraising category. It is the practice itself.

Maharaj's central teaching was that no being is separate from God, and that God is present in each and every person and in all things around us. Held seriously, that teaching does not permit a distinction between the person you bow to in the sanctum and the person you hand a blanket to on Monroe Street. Both are the same. The aarti and the food drive are one practice, not two.

This is why the mandir's calendar looks the way it does. Alongside the daily upasana and the three great utsav of the year sit the food drives, the winter blanket and clothing distribution, the school supply collection, the toy drive and the hygiene kits. None of these are outreach programmes bolted onto a place of worship. They are what the worship is for.

## What we do

**Daily upasana.** Aarti is offered every day of the year — Bhupali at first light, Shejarati at the close. The mandir is open for darshan on Thursday, Saturday and Sunday; on every other day the seva continues online, and devotees join from wherever they are.

**Utsav.** Three principal observances: Pragat Din, marking Maharaj's appearance in Shegaon on 23 February 1878; Guru Pournima; and Rishi Panchami, the day he took Samadhi. Monthly upasana falls on the first Sunday of each month.

**Community service.** Food drives in partnership with local Bay Area food banks. Winter blankets and warm clothing through partner shelters in San Jose. School supplies at the start of the academic year. Toys each December. Hygiene kits year-round.

**Youth seva.** High school students earn verified community service hours through genuine work — event logistics, crowd management, food service coordination, supply chain support and administrative seva — with formal verification letters issued for graduation requirements and college applications.

## How we are run

The mandir has no paid staff. Every aarti sung, every meal cooked, every blanket packed and every line of this website is volunteer seva, given by devotees around their jobs and their families.

Funds are held and disbursed by the board of trustees, and every gift is receipted. We publish what we spend, because a community that gives its money to a cause is owed an account of where it went.

## Everyone is welcome

You do not need to be Hindu. You do not need to read Marathi, know any of the prayers, or have set foot in a mandir before. Nothing will be asked of you and nothing is expected.

Come on a Thursday evening or a weekend afternoon, leave your shoes at the door, sit at the back for as long as you like, and take prasad before you go. That is the whole of it.
